AI Ops Toolkit
AI Drafts. I Verify.

Three reusable prompts for core EBP work: a meeting summary, a board-prep brief, and an SOP. Each one went through several rounds of testing and review until it was reliable.

The Setup

The Scenario

Northpine Labs is a fictional remote-first B2B SaaS company: Series B, 140 employees, about $9M in ARR, and 20 months of runway.

I worked as EBP to the CEO, starting from a messy, auto-generated transcript of a weekly exec staff meeting. It covered a $90K burn overage, a slipping AI feature that put a $180K deal at risk, a missing procurement process, and a disagreement about what to tell the company at the all-hands meeting.

All company names, people, and figures are fictional, created for practice and portfolio use.

Deliverable 1

Exec Meeting Summary

A private 3-line TL;DR for the CEO, plus a Slack-ready recap for the exec team: decisions, risks, unresolved items, and action items with owners and dates.

What the first draft got wrong

  • Missed the board deck build, a task assigned to "Dana's EBP," which meant my own deliverable
  • Missed a legal review mentioned as a statement ("we need legal to look at this")
  • Implied the win rate drop caused a revenue risk, which nobody said in the meeting

What I changed

  • Gave the AI my identity and the company context, so "Dana's EBP" mapped to a name
  • Added a rule to capture implied tasks and flag them as [OWNER UNKNOWN]
  • Added "Prove the Decision": proposals go under Unresolved, not Decisions
Result: a reusable weekly template. Swap in the transcript and attendee list, and it converts relative dates ("by Thursday") to real dates, tags missing owners, and keeps proposals separate from decisions.
Deliverable 2

CEO / CFO Board-Prep Brief

A one-page brief for the CEO's 1:1 with the CFO, built from the meeting transcript and the September financial dashboard.

Built to Drive Decisions

  • The CEO leaves with three decisions: how to frame the misses, which runway number to commit to, and who owns each slide
  • Questions for the CFO focus on what's still unknown, not what the CEO already heard

What the Math Surfaced

  • Runway: $23.1M ÷ $1.15M burn ≈ 20 months
  • A 22-month commitment requires burn of about $1.05M, which is below plan
  • That turned a vague goal into a concrete board-level tradeoff

What Review Caught

  • My own timeline had the wrong weekdays (Oct 12 listed as a Sunday)
  • Fix: a rule that makes the AI flag conflicting inputs instead of quietly picking one
Lesson: AI repeats your mistakes with confidence. The EBP is the fact-checker.
Deliverable 3

Software & Vendor Request SOP

A procurement SOP drafted for CFO review, designed to close the gaps that let two purchases skip approval.

Controls I Specified

  • Approval tiers based on annual contract value: VP, CFO, CEO
  • Security review based on data access, not cost
  • No corporate card purchases without an approved request
  • A 24-hour fast track, so urgent requests go through the process instead of around it

Guardrails on the AI

  • No invented teams, people, or dollar amounts
  • Unknowns marked [TO CONFIRM], which turned into a ready-made agenda for the CFO review
  • Stays in scope: the first draft had added an unrelated sales policy

Stress Test

  • Ran both real incidents through the new process step by step
  • Card purchase: flagged at monthly close
  • Agency invoice: held until an approval and contract are on file

Excerpt from the final prompt's rules:

Rules: 1. Do not invent teams, roles, or people beyond those listed above. 2. If something is needed but not specified, mark it [TO CONFIRM] instead of making it up. 3. Stay in scope: do not add policies unrelated to procurement. 4. No bureaucratic steps that would push people back to Slack DMs. 5. After the SOP, add a "Stress Test": walk both recent purchases through the new process and show where each would have been caught.
How I Work With AI

My Review Process

1 · Frame

Role, context, decision

Start with who I am, who the audience is, and what decision the output must support.

2 · Draft

AI writes v1

Fast and clean, but it only catches what was said explicitly.

3 · Verify

Check against the source

Implied tasks, wrong owners, invented causes, and date math.

4 · Harden

Turn fixes into rules

Every miss becomes a rule, so the next run gets it right.
