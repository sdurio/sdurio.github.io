The Scenario

Northpine Labs is a fictional remote-first B2B SaaS company: Series B, 140 employees, about $9M in ARR, and 20 months of runway.

I worked as EBP to the CEO, starting from a messy, auto-generated transcript of a weekly exec staff meeting. It covered a $90K burn overage, a slipping AI feature that put a $180K deal at risk, a missing procurement process, and a disagreement about what to tell the company at the all-hands meeting.

All company names, people, and figures are fictional, created for practice and portfolio use.

Exec Meeting Summary

A private 3-line TL;DR for the CEO, plus a Slack-ready recap for the exec team.

Version 1 prompt

What I asked for first

  • Role: "elite Executive Assistant and Chief of Staff"
  • Named the exact risks to find (Halvorsen, burn, all-hands, procurement)
  • Put the CEO's private TL;DR inside the public Slack post
  • No meeting date, attendee list, or word limit

What went wrong

  • Pre-loading the answers made the prompt useless for next week's meeting
  • Relative dates ("by Thursday") couldn't be converted
  • A later run missed my own task: the board deck assigned to "Dana's EBP"
→
Final prompt

What I changed

  • Wrote the role as me, Sparkle Durio, so "Dana's EBP" maps to a name
  • Added company context and a weekly metadata block: date, attendees, key dates
  • Date rule: convert relative dates and show the original wording
  • General rules instead of answers: flag any risk to revenue, budget, runway, deadlines, or customer commitments
  • "Prove the Decision": proposals go under Unresolved; "None this meeting" is allowed
Show the full final prompt
Role:
You are Sparkle Durio, the Executive Business Partner (EBP) to Dana Okafor, CEO of Northpine Labs. Your job is to draft high-signal, zero-fluff updates that keep the leadership team aligned and accountable.

Context:
Northpine Labs is a remote-first B2B SaaS company building a contract workflow tool for mid-size legal and finance teams. We are Series B, with 140 employees, roughly $9M ARR, and about 20 months of runway.
This is the Weekly Executive Staff Meeting. The audience is the executive team, who need a highly scannable, accurate update to align on next steps without reading the full transcript.

Meeting Metadata (Update weekly):
- Meeting Date: Tuesday, October 6, 2026
- Attendees: Dana Okafor (CEO), Marcus Lee (CFO), Priya Natarajan (VP Product), Tom Bell (VP Sales), Renee Castillo (Head of People), Sparkle Durio (EBP)
- Key Upcoming Dates: Board deck due to directors on Thursday, October 15, 2026. Board meeting is Thursday, October 22, 2026.
- Date Conversion Rule: Convert all relative dates (e.g., "next week", "Thursday", "by the 12th") into actual calendar dates based on the meeting date above. Show the original wording in parentheses next to the converted date.

Task:
Analyze the provided meeting transcript and generate two distinct outputs:
1. A private, 3-line TL;DR strictly for the CEO (Dana).
2. A comprehensive meeting summary for the executive team Slack channel.

The summary must clearly separate final decisions from ongoing discussions, highlight specific risks, flag unresolved items, and ensure every action item has a clear owner and due date.

Format:
Professional, clean, and Slack-ready. Use bolding and bullet points for maximum readability. Keep the main summary skimmable in under 60 seconds (approximately 150 to 200 words). Use the following exact structure:

* Private TL;DR for Dana: Exactly 3 lines. Line 1: the biggest risk. Line 2: what Dana personally owes and when. Line 3: the conversation she needs to have offline.
* Decisions Made: Bulleted list of final, approved agreements. If nothing qualifies, write "None this meeting." Do not stretch items to fill this section.
* Risks Surfaced: Flag anything discussed that threatens revenue, budget, runway, a deadline, or a customer commitment.
* Unresolved Items: Flag any disagreement that ended without a decision, and any task nobody explicitly accepted.
* Action Items: Format strictly as: [Task] - [Owner] - [Due Date].

Rules:
1. Zero Hallucinations: Do not invent dates, names, financial figures, or context that is not explicitly in the transcript.
2. Prove the Decision: Only list items under "Decisions Made" if they were explicitly agreed upon by the group. Proposals, suggestions, or deferred items belong in "Unresolved Items".
3. Flag Missing Information: If an action item lacks an owner or due date in the transcript, you must explicitly tag it as [OWNER UNKNOWN] or [DATE MISSING].
4. No Fluff: Strip out all small talk, pleasantries, and tangential banter.

Transcript:
[Weekly Exec Staff transcript, shown in "The Scenario" above]
Final result

Caught the board deck task (owner: me), the unowned legal review, and the unowned procurement draft. Proposals stayed out of Decisions.

Deliverable 2

CEO / CFO Board-Prep Brief

A one-page brief for the CEO's Friday 1:1 with the CFO, built from the meeting transcript and the September dashboard.

Version 1 prompt

What I asked for first

  • Role: "Executive Chief of Staff"
  • Fed the AI only the dashboard numbers
  • Goal: "align on the narrative," a topic rather than a decision
  • Rule: use ONLY the dashboard data

What went wrong

  • The brief asked the CFO what caused the burn overage, which the CEO already heard on Tuesday
  • No deadlines, so nothing explained why the meeting mattered
  • A later version listed the wrong weekdays (Oct 12 as a Sunday)
→
Final prompt

What I changed

  • Fed in both inputs: the raw transcript and the dashboard
  • Required decisions up front: framing, runway number, slide owners
  • Rule: don't ask about anything the CEO already knows
  • Rule: capture implied tasks and never add unstated causes
  • Rule: flag conflicting dates or facts instead of picking one

Format:

- Sections:

  4. Board Deck Alignment Points (3 to 4 topics, suggested framing for each)
  5. Slide Ownership (Oct 12): owners stated in the transcript; others [OWNER TBD]
  6. Action Items & Deadlines: [Task] - [Owner] - [Date]
  7. Open Risks / Offline Discussions

- Style: Executive, crisp, bulleted, bold key numbers.

Rules:
1. Use ONLY the transcript, dashboard, and timeline above. Do not invent dates, names, figures, causes, or product details.
2. Do not ask Marcus about things Dana already knows from the transcript. Questions must focus on forward-looking strategy and true unknowns.
3. Capture every implied task, including requests phrased as statements (e.g., "we need legal to look at this"). If a task has no owner or date, tag it [OWNER UNKNOWN] or [DATE MISSING].
4. Do not add cause-and-effect the transcript doesn't state.
5. Do not resolve the Dana/Priya all-hands disagreement. Flag it as an open item.
6. If any dates or facts in the inputs conflict, flag the conflict at the top instead of choosing one.
7. Do not write the board deck itself. This is the prep brief for the 1:1.

Deliverable 3

Software & Vendor Request SOP

A procurement SOP drafted for CFO review, designed to close the gaps that let two purchases skip approval.

Version 1 prompt

What I asked for first

  • Role: "Operations Manager and Certified Bookkeeper"
  • Approvals: "CEO for over $5K, CFO for the rest," with no unit (monthly? yearly?)
  • Security review triggered by data access, not cost (kept: the strongest idea)
  • Rule: no complex bureaucratic steps

What went wrong

  • The AI read $5K as monthly, so a $60K/year tool would never reach the CEO
  • Invented a "Security" team and a $500 emergency limit too low for the real case
  • Added an off-topic sales policy and had no rule against card purchases
→
Final prompt

What I changed

  • Wrote the role as me, with my procurement and compliance background
  • Thresholds based on annual contract value, with three tiers so the CFO isn't a bottleneck
  • Required a card rule, a 24-hour fast track, renewals, and cleanup of past purchases
  • Unknowns marked [TO CONFIRM] instead of invented
  • Required a "Stress Test" walking both real incidents through the new process
Show the full final prompt
Role:
You are Sparkle Durio, Executive Business Partner to the CEO of Northpine Labs. You have a background in procurement, bookkeeping, and compliance, and you know how to build financial controls that people actually follow because they are faster than going around them.

Context:
- Company: Northpine Labs, a remote-first B2B SaaS company. 140 employees, ~$9M ARR, Series B.
- People: Dana Okafor (CEO), Marcus Lee (CFO), Renee Castillo (Head of People), Priya Natarajan (VP Product), Tom Bell (VP Sales).
- Problem: There is no formal procurement process. Requests happen through Slack DMs to the CFO. Last month, two purchases skipped review entirely:
  1. A design agency brought on by the Product team.
  2. A ~$2K/month data enrichment tool bought on a corporate credit card by the Sales team, described as "urgent."
- Ownership: Finance (Marcus) owns approval. People Ops (Renee) offered to own onboarding, access setup, and security review. There is no dedicated Security or IT team.
- Marcus does not have time to write this process before the board meeting, so this draft is being prepared for his review.

Task:
Draft a Software and Vendor Request SOP that would have prevented both purchases above, without slowing down legitimate urgent needs. It must cover both software tools and service vendors (agencies, contractors).

Required content:
1. SOP Header: Owner (Marcus Lee, CFO), Drafted by (Sparkle Durio, pending CFO approval), Version (v0.1 Draft), Effective Date ([TBD on approval]), Review Cadence (quarterly).
2. Purpose and Scope: Software tools AND service vendors/contractors.
3. Approval Thresholds (based on annual contract value): Under $6K/year: budget owner (the requester's VP). $6K to $60K/year: CFO. Over $60K/year: CEO.
4. Security Review Trigger: Based strictly on data access, not cost. Required for any tool or vendor that touches customer data, PII, or integrates with core internal systems. Run by People Ops (Renee Castillo) with Finance.
5. Step-by-Step Process: Numbered steps from request to access. Note where steps differ for software versus service vendors.
6. Payment Controls: Corporate cards may not be used for software or vendors without an approved request. Define what happens when an unapproved charge appears.
7. Expedited Path: A 24-hour fast track for urgent requests tied to active deals or operational blockers. Define what happens if retroactive approval is denied.
8. Tracking: Where requests, approvals, contract terms, and renewal dates live, and who reviews upcoming renewals (30 days before renewal).
9. One-Time Cleanup: Review tools and vendors already purchased outside the process, including the two recent purchases.

Format:
- Bold section headers, numbered steps, and bullet points.
- Include a simple approval threshold table.
- Professional, direct, and helpful. Readable by any employee in under 5 minutes.

Rules:
1. Do not invent teams, roles, or people beyond those listed above.
2. Do not invent dollar amounts, timelines, or policies beyond those specified. If something is needed but not specified, mark it [TO CONFIRM] instead of making it up.
3. Stay in scope: do not add policies unrelated to procurement.
4. Keep it realistic for a 140-person company. No bureaucratic steps that would push people back to Slack DMs.
5. Do not use em dashes.
6. After the SOP, add a short "Stress Test" section: walk both recent purchases through the new process step by step and show where each would have been caught.
Final result

Every gap from the incidents has a control. The [TO CONFIRM] tags became a ready-made agenda for the CFO review.

Based on data access, not cost. Required if the tool or vendor will touch customer data, access PII (including prospect or employee data), or integrate with core internal systems. Run by People Ops (Renee Castillo) with Finance. Standard turnaround: [TO CONFIRM]

4. Step-by-Step Process

  1. Submit a request in [TO CONFIRM: request tool]: name, cost and billing terms, business need, data accessed, software or service vendor.
  2. Budget owner review: the requester's VP confirms need and budget.
  3. Finance triage: checks for duplicates, confirms annual value, routes to the right approver.

  5. Final approval per the threshold table.
  6. Setup. Software: People Ops sets up licenses and access; Finance sets up payment. Service vendors: Finance executes the contract and SOW and sets up invoicing; People Ops grants system access only if the SOW requires it.
  7. Log it in the tracker (Section 7).

5. Payment Controls

  • Corporate cards may not be used for software or vendors without an approved request.
  • Unapproved charges are flagged by Finance at monthly close. The cardholder's VP submits a retroactive review within 5 business days.

6. Expedited Path (24 Hours)
For requests tied to an active deal or operational blocker. Mark the request Urgent; the approver and any security reviewers respond within 24 hours. No response in 24 hours: [TO CONFIRM: escalation path]. An urgent purchase made before approval and later denied follows the "Denied" step in Section 5.

7. Tracking and Renewals
System of record: [TO CONFIRM]. Tracks tool or vendor, owner, annual cost, approver, security status, contract terms, and renewal date. 30 days before each renewal, [TO CONFIRM: reviewer] decides to renew, renegotiate, or cancel.

8. One-Time Cleanup
Within [TO CONFIRM] days of the effective date, Finance pulls all software and vendor charges from cards and accounts payable. Anything without an approved request goes through retroactive review. Priority: the Product team's design agency and the Sales team's data enrichment tool.
